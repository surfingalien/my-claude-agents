# Ideation Frameworks

## SCAMPER

A checklist of transformations to apply to any existing idea or product:

| Letter | Action | Question |
|--------|--------|----------|
| **S** | Substitute | What can be substituted? (materials, people, processes) |
| **C** | Combine | What can be combined with this? |
| **A** | Adapt | What can be adapted from another context? |
| **M** | Modify / Magnify | What can be changed, enlarged, or exaggerated? |
| **P** | Put to other uses | Can this serve a different purpose or user? |
| **E** | Eliminate | What can be removed or simplified? |
| **R** | Reverse / Rearrange | What if we reversed the order or approach? |

**Usage:** Apply each letter to your current idea and generate at least one response per letter. Discard most; keep the generative ones.

## How Might We (HMW)

Reframe problems as possibility questions:

**From:** "Users don't complete onboarding"
**To HMW:**
- HMW make step 1 so compelling users rush to step 2?
- HMW reduce onboarding to a single step?
- HMW give value before onboarding completes?

**Formula:** "How might we [achieve goal] for [user] so that [outcome]?"

**Tips:**
- Too broad: "HMW improve everything?" (not useful)
- Too narrow: "HMW add a progress bar?" (already a solution)
- Just right: "HMW make users feel progress after each step?"

## First Principles

Strip away assumptions and rebuild from fundamentals:

1. **State the current approach** — "We build to-do apps with lists and tags"
2. **Ask why, repeatedly** — Why lists? Why tags? Why mobile-first?
3. **Identify the fundamental truth** — "Users need to not forget things and act on them"
4. **Rebuild from that truth** — What's the simplest system that solves forgetting + acting?

**When to use:** When you're improving something that might be fundamentally broken. When iteration has hit a ceiling.

## Jobs to Be Done (JTBD)

Focus on the job the user is hiring the product to do:

**Format:** "When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [outcome]."

**Example:**
- "When I have a busy day ahead, I want to see only what truly matters, so I can feel in control without being overwhelmed."

**Discovery questions:**
- What were they doing before your product?
- What's the first thing they do after they "hire" your product?
- What would make them "fire" your product?

## Constraint-Based Ideation

Use artificial constraints to force creative solutions:

- "Solve this with no UI" → What if it was just an email?
- "Solve this for $0 budget" → What changes when you can't buy your way out?
- "Solve this in 24 hours" → What's the MVP of the MVP?
- "Solve this for a 10-year-old" → What's the simplest possible version?
- "Solve this for 10x the users" → What breaks at scale?

---

## Pre-mortem

Before committing to an idea, imagine it has already failed:

1. "It's six months later and this idea completely failed."
2. "Write down every reason why it failed."
3. Review the list — which risks are real and preventable?
4. Adjust the idea or the plan to address the real risks.

**Anti-pattern:** Optimism bias causes teams to skip this. The pre-mortem forces pessimism at the right time.

---

## Analogous Inspiration

Find how adjacent industries solve similar problems:

**Your problem:** "Users don't return to the app after initial signup"
**Analogous problems:**
- How do gyms retain members? → Streak mechanics, community, sunk cost
- How do newspapers retain subscribers? → Daily digest, personalisation, habit formation
- How do games retain players? → Progression systems, social proof, daily rewards

Pick one analogy. Extract the mechanism. Ask if it applies.
