# Psychologist Skill

## Overview

Provides psychological frameworks for building credible characters, analyzing interpersonal dynamics, and modeling realistic human behavior under stress, trauma, and change. Grounded in Big Five personality theory, attachment theory, psychodynamic defense mechanisms, cognitive behavioral patterns, developmental psychology (Erikson, Bowlby), and social psychology. Built for fiction writing, narrative design, game development, and human behavior analysis.

## Capabilities

### Psychological Analysis Workflow

```
1. Observe before diagnosing: Gather behavioral evidence first
   - What does this person DO? (not what they say about themselves)
   - Behavior under stress is more diagnostic than baseline behavior

2. Use multiple lenses simultaneously:
   - No single theory explains everything
   - Cross-reference Big Five with attachment theory with cultural context

3. Check for stereotypes:
   - Is this a real psychological pattern or a Hollywood shorthand?
   - "Genius with social deficits" "trauma = broken" "evil for evil's sake"

4. Trace behavior to origin:
   - What developmental experience or belief system drives this behavior?
   - The behavior makes sense from inside the character's internal logic

5. Project forward realistically:
   - Given this psychology, what would this person do under specific circumstances?
   - Not what would be dramatically convenient
```

### Interpersonal Dynamics Analysis Template

```markdown
RELATIONAL DYNAMICS: [Character A] ↔ [Character B]
===================================================
Model: Attachment / Transactional Analysis / Drama Triangle / Power Analysis

Power Dynamic: Symmetrical / Complementary / Shifting
  - Symmetrical: roughly equal power (peer relationships, siblings)
  - Complementary: one dominant, one subordinate (parent/child model replicated in adult relationships)

Communication Pattern:
  - Overt: What they say to each other
  - Covert: What's actually communicated beneath the words
  - Blocked topics: What they cannot discuss directly

Unspoken Contract:
  - What A implicitly expects from B (never articulated)
  - What B implicitly expects from A (never articulated)
  - Where the contract is broken and what it costs

Trigger Points:
  - What specific behavior from B triggers A's attachment/wound?
  - What specific behavior from A triggers B's attachment/wound?
  - The escalation pattern when both triggers fire simultaneously

Drama Triangle (Karpman):
  - Roles: Persecutor / Rescuer / Victim (and how they rotate)
  - Which character defaults to which role?
  - What event causes role switches?

Growth Edge: [What a healthier version of this relationship would look like]
  - What would each person need to change?
  - What new contract would need to be negotiated?
```

### Personality Frameworks Reference

**Big Five (OCEAN) — Research-Backed Basis**
```
Why Big Five vs. MBTI:
- Big Five: derived from factor analysis of thousands of personality descriptors
- MBTI: 
- Big Five better predicts behavior, job performance, relationship outcomes
- MBTI useful as narrative shorthand only — not psychological truth

Big Five in character design:
High O + Low C: Creative, scattered, unreliable but fascinating (artist archetype)
High C + Low O: Disciplined, conventional, reliable but inflexible (soldier archetype)
High E + High A: Warm, social, popular, potentially conflict-avoidant
Low E + High N: Withdrawn, anxious, internally rich, socially draining
Low A + High N: Difficult, combative, volatile — villain potential without care
```

**Attachment Theory (Bowlby, Ainsworth, Main)**
```
Formed by: Early caregiver relationship (0-2 years)
Affects: All subsequent intimate relationships throughout life

Secure (60% of adults):
- Comfortable with intimacy and autonomy
- Can ask for help, can give it, not threatened by closeness
- Conflict: direct, non-defensive, repairs quickly

Anxious-Preoccupied (20%):
- Hyper-vigilant to rejection signals
- Clings, monitors, escalates to get reassurance
- Fears abandonment → behavior that drives away → confirms fear
- Partner's distance = threat; closeness never feels enough

Dismissive-Avoidant (25%):
- Self-reliant to the point of shutting others out
- Discomfort with intimacy, difficulty depending on others
- Appears cold, self-sufficient, emotionally unavailable
- Closeness → triggers avoidance → partner escalates → avoidance increases

Fearful-Avoidant (Disorganized, 5%):
- Wants closeness AND fears it simultaneously
- Inconsistent: pursues, then withdraws when closeness achieved
- Associated with early trauma, especially from caregivers
- Most complex and destructive in adult relationships
```

**Trauma Response Diversity**
```
NOT all trauma responses are the same. Common fictional mistake: trauma = quiet/broken.

Hypervigilance:
- Constant scanning for threat, startles easily
- Interprets ambiguous signals as threatening
- Exhausting to self and others
- Often looks like aggression or paranoia

Fawning/People-pleasing:
- Compliance as survival strategy (threat = disapproval)
- Difficulty knowing own preferences ("what do YOU want?")
- High empathy, reads others constantly
- Resentment builds when others don't reciprocate

Compartmentalization:
- Trauma filed away, high functioning in normal life
- The "fine, I'm fine" response — genuinely functional until trigger
- Sudden, seemingly disproportionate reactions when compartment breaches

Dissociation:
- Ranges from mild "zoning out" to full dissociative identity
- Depersonalization (watching self from outside), derealization (world feels unreal)
- Memory gaps, especially for traumatic periods

Hyperarousal vs. Hypoarousal:
- Hyperarousal: can't sleep, easily startled, emotional volatility
- Hypoarousal: numbness, disconnection, can't feel much
- Both are nervous system dysregulation responses to threat
```

**Cognitive Distortions (Beck/Burns)**
```
All-or-nothing thinking: "If I'm not perfect, I'm a failure"
Catastrophizing: "This setback means everything will fall apart"
Mind reading: "They didn't reply because they hate me"
Fortune telling: "This will definitely go wrong"
Personalization: "Their bad mood is because of me"
Emotional reasoning: "I feel stupid, therefore I am stupid"
Should statements: "I should never feel angry" → guilt when feeling angry
Labeling: "I'm a loser" not "I made a mistake"
Filtering: Only notices evidence that confirms the negative belief
Magnification/Minimization: Magnifies failures, minimizes successes
```

## Scripts

### `scripts/psychological_profile_builder.py`

Builds a structured psychological profile from behavioral descriptions, using Big Five and attachment theory frameworks.

```
Usage: python psychological_profile_builder.py character.json [--format json|report]
Input JSON: name, age, behaviors[], key_relationships[], backstory_events[],
            observed_reactions{stress, intimacy, conflict, success, failure}
Output:
  - Big Five trait estimates with behavioral evidence
  - Attachment style assessment with relationship pattern description
  - Primary defense mechanisms
  - Core wound hypothesis (most parsimonious explanation)
  - Cognitive distortion profile
  - Consistency check: do the pieces fit together?
```

### `scripts/relational_dynamics_mapper.py`

Maps psychological dynamics between two characters, identifying power patterns, communication styles, and escalation triggers.

```
Usage: python relational_dynamics_mapper.py char_a.json char_b.json [--relationship-type romantic|family|work|friendship]
Input: Psychological profiles for both characters
Output:
  - Attachment compatibility analysis (which styles interact how)
  - Drama Triangle role assignments and rotation patterns
  - Trigger/escalation cycle map
  - Unspoken contract identification
  - Conflict resolution capacity assessment
  - Growth edge recommendations
```

### `scripts/trauma_response_modeler.py`

Models realistic trauma responses for specified traumatic events, avoiding cliché and Hollywood shorthand.

```
Usage: python trauma_response_modeler.py --trauma-type [childhood_neglect|acute_violence|loss|betrayal|systemic]
                                          --personality-base character.json
                                          --time-since [immediate|weeks|years|decades]
Output:
  - Primary response style (hypervigilance / fawning / compartmentalization / dissociation)
  - Specific behavioral manifestations at each time point
  - Trigger identification (what activates the trauma response?)
  - Functional areas affected vs. preserved
  - Realistic recovery trajectory
  - Common misconceptions to avoid
```

## Quality Standards

- Psychological observations cite specific frameworks (not "they seem insecure" but "anxious-preoccupied attachment manifesting as...")
- Character profiles include both adaptive and maladaptive patterns — no one is purely broken
- Interpersonal dynamics identify specific trigger mechanisms, not vague "they don't get along"
- Cultural and contextual factors acknowledged when relevant
- Limitations of applied frameworks stated honestly (replication crisis, cultural bias, etc.)
- Trauma responses are diverse — not all trauma produces the same presentation
